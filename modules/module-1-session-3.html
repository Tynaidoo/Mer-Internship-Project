Onboarding - Understanding the Company Handbook

Understand onboarding, company policies and workplace procedures, including where to find important information and who to approach when you are unsure what to do.

Onboarding • Policies • Procedures • Confidentiality

Purpose

To introduce interns to onboarding, company policies and workplace procedures and help them understand where to find important information and who to approach when they are unsure what to do.

Learning Outcomes

By the end of the session, interns will be able to:

  • Explain what onboarding is.
  • Explain why organisations have policies and procedures.
  • Locate important information in their company's handbook.
  • Identify basic expectations around attendance, leave and workplace behaviour.
  • Recognise the importance of confidentiality and data protection.
  • Identify appropriate use of company technology and social media.
Spark

Imagine you are unsure whether you are allowed to do something at work. What would you do first? Where would you look? Who would you ask?

What is onboarding?

Onboarding is the process of helping a new employee understand the organisation, their role, their team and how work gets done.

Onboarding can include:

1

Organisation

Understanding the company, its purpose, culture and structure.

2

Your role

Learning your responsibilities, expectations and priorities.

3

People & team

Meeting colleagues, supervisors and the people you will work with.

4

Policies & systems

Learning the procedures, tools, rules and systems used in the workplace.

Being reliable means being where you are expected to be, when you are expected to be there. If something prevents you from attending work or meeting your expected hours, follow the company's procedure and communicate with the appropriate person.

The table below highlights a few generic examples of company policies.

PolicyExplanation
Attendance & working hoursBeing reliable means being where you are expected to be, when you are expected to be there. If something prevents you from attending work or meeting your expected hours, follow the company's procedure and communicate with the appropriate person.
Leave and time offUnderstand how leave is requested, who approves it and how much notice is required. Never assume that simply telling a colleague means your leave has been approved.
Dress and appearanceWorkplace expectations differ across organisations and industries. Understand what is appropriate for your particular workplace rather than assuming that one dress standard applies everywhere.
Workplace behaviourProfessional behaviour means treating colleagues, clients and other stakeholders with respect. It includes how you speak to people, behave in meetings, use company resources and respond to disagreement.
Health and safetyYou are responsible for following health and safety procedures and reporting hazards, incidents or concerns through the appropriate channels.
ConfidentialityInformation you encounter at work may not be yours to share. Confidential information can include client information, internal documents, financial information, passwords, business plans and personal information.
Data protection and privacyPersonal and sensitive information should only be accessed, used or shared for legitimate work purposes and in accordance with company policy and applicable law.
TechnologyCompany devices, accounts, systems and networks are provided for work-related purposes according to company policy. Keep passwords secure and use systems responsibly.
Social mediaRemember that your online activity can affect how others perceive you and, in some situations, may affect the organisation. Follow the company's social media policy and do not share confidential information.
Respectful workplace / anti-harassmentEveryone has the right to a workplace where they are treated with dignity and respect. Harassment, bullying, discrimination and inappropriate conduct should not be ignored. Know the channels available for raising concerns.

Why do companies have policies?

Rather than viewing policies as a list of “rules you must obey”, understand their purpose.

Policies help organisations:

  • Create consistent expectations.
  • Protect employees and the organisation.
  • Support fairness.
  • Protect confidential information.
  • Maintain health and safety.
  • Meet legal and regulatory requirements.
  • Explain what to do in common situations.
  • Provide processes for dealing with problems.

Activity: The Company Handbook

Activity

Explore your company handbook

Take a look at your company’s handbook. Using your handbook, answer the following questions.

  1. What are the normal working hours?
  2. Who should you contact if you are sick?
  3. Where do you find the leave procedure?
  4. What does the company say about confidentiality?
  5. Who can you contact if you experience inappropriate behaviour?

Checklist

  • I am able to explain what onboarding is.
  • I am able to explain why organisations have policies and procedures.
  • I can locate important information in my company's handbook.
  • I can identify basic expectations around attendance, leave and workplace behaviour.
  • I can recognise the importance of confidentiality and data protection.
  • I am able to identify appropriate use of company technology and social media.

Reflection

Reflect

After exploring the handbook, what is one policy or procedure you think you should become familiar with before starting your first week? Why?
