Collaboration & Team Dynamics

Learn to collaborate with people who bring different personalities, perspectives, communication styles and responsibilities to the workplace.

Collaboration • Team Dynamics • Conflict • Trust

Purpose

To prepare interns to work effectively with people who have different personalities, perspectives, communication styles and responsibilities.
Time allocation: 45–60 minutes

Learning outcomes

Interns should be able to:

  • Explain why collaboration matters.
  • Identify effective team behaviours.
  • Recognise the difference between contributing and dominating.
  • Demonstrate respectful disagreement.
  • Identify ways to build trust.
  • Recognise the importance of roles and responsibilities.
Spark

What makes someone a good team member? Is it more important to be helpful, competent, agreeable, outspoken or reliable? Why?

Navigating Collaboration Effectively

Coordinated Collaboration

Core principle: Teams thrive on shared goals, clear ownership, and transparent communication—not uniform behaviour.

  • Align on clear objectives and clarify role ownership.
  • Map out dependencies and maintain communication.
  • Set team norms for listening, challenging, and decision-making to leverage diverse working styles.

Contributing vs. Dominating

Core principle: True collaboration balances sharing value with making space for other voices.

  • Step Up: Offer ideas, evidence, questions, and support when your input adds value.
  • Step Back: Avoid interrupting, dismissing alternatives, or making unilateral choices.
  • Practise intentional speaking management to encourage balanced participation.

Disagreement & Trust

Core principle: Disagreement can be productive when focused on the work; trust builds through consistency and respect.

  • Focus debate on evidence, assumptions, options, and consequences.
  • Build trust by being honest about uncertainty and remaining respectful under pressure.
  • Apply the 5-Step Process: Acknowledge → Clarify → Test assumptions → Propose options → Agree on next steps.

Handling Disagreements & Building Trust

Disagreement in teams is natural and, when managed correctly, can lead to better outcomes. It is useful to distinguish between task conflict and relationship conflict.

Frameworks for Healthy Collaboration

  • Thomas-Kilmann Conflict Model: Choosing the right approach—Collaborating, Compromising, Accommodating, Avoiding or Competing—based on context.
  • Separating Ideas from Identity: Challenge an idea constructively, for example, “Have we considered scenario X?”, without attacking the person.
  • Building Psychological Safety: Acknowledge mistakes, validate others' input and maintain confidentiality.
👂 Acknowledge → 🔍 Clarify → 🧠 Test Assumptions → 💡 Propose Options → 🤝 Agree Next Step

The Trust Equation

In high-performance environments, trust is a measurable outcome of daily interactions. The Trust Equation breaks team trust into credibility, reliability, emotional connection and self-orientation. Credibility, reliability and connection strengthen trust, while high self-orientation can weaken it. Understanding these elements can help teams create a more cohesive, high-trust culture where collaboration and performance thrive.

Activity — What’s Your Conflict Style?

Use the Thomas-Kilmann Conflict Model to identify different approaches to workplace conflict and decide which approach is most appropriate in different situations.

  • Competing – I assert my position and focus on getting my way.
  • Collaborating – We work together to find a solution that satisfies both sides.
  • Compromising – We each give up something to reach an acceptable solution.
  • Avoiding – I step away from or postpone the conflict.
  • Accommodating – I prioritise the other person's needs or preferences.
