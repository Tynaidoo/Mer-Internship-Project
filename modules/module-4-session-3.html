Module 4 • Session 3

Project Management

Learn how to plan, organise and monitor workplace projects from an initial idea through to a completed outcome.

Planning • Project Life Cycle • Risk • Monitoring

Purpose of the Session

The purpose of this session is to introduce you to basic principles of project management and equip you with practical tools for planning, organising and monitoring workplace projects. The session will help you understand how projects move from an idea to a completed outcome and how individual responsibilities contribute to successful project delivery.

Learning Outcomes

By the end of the session, you should be able to:

  • Define a project and explain how projects differ from routine work.
  • Identify the key stages of a project.
  • Identify project goals, deliverables, tasks and deadlines.
  • Break a project into manageable tasks.
  • Use basic project management tools to monitor progress.
Time allocation: 1 hour

SPARK: You Have Been Given a Project!

Scenario

Your supervisor tells you that your team must organise a virtual internship event for 50 interns in three weeks. You have never managed an event before. Where would you start?

Think about:

  • What would you need to know?
  • Who would need to be involved?
  • What needs to happen first?
  • What could go wrong?
  • How would you know whether the project was successful?

What Is Project Management?

Routine work

Repeated regularly, usually follows established processes and has no defined end point.

A project

Has a specific objective, a beginning and an end, and produces a defined deliverable or outcome.

Project delivery

Requires resources, planning, coordination, responsibilities and monitoring.

For interns, projects could include:

  • Developing a presentation.
  • Organising an event.
  • Conducting research.
  • Creating a training resource.
  • Developing a communication campaign.
  • Collecting and analysing data.
  • Designing a website or digital resource.

The Project Life Cycle

A simple project life cycle has five stages:

1. InitiateWhat are we trying to achieve?
2. PlanHow will we achieve it?
3. ExecuteComplete the planned work.
4. Monitor & ControlAre we on track? What needs to change?
5. CloseWas it completed successfully, and what did we learn?

Project management is not just planning. Monitoring and adapting are equally important.

The Project Management Triangle

Consider the relationship between:

Scope

What needs to be done?

Time

When must it be done?

Resources / Cost

What resources do we have?

Changing one part of a project can affect the others.

If your supervisor adds five additional deliverables but keeps the same deadline, the team may need additional people, resources or a different approach.

Managing Project Risks

Good project managers anticipate problems before they happen.

Technology failsMeeting disruptedTest technology and have a backup

Instructions are unclearIncorrect workClarify expectations early

What could go wrong, and what can we do about it before it happens?

Activity 4.3.2: The Project Is Falling Behind

Scenario

You are part of an internship team responsible for creating a digital onboarding guide for new interns. The guide must be completed in two weeks.

Your team has five members:

  • One person is responsible for researching content.
  • One is responsible for writing.
  • One is responsible for graphic design.
  • One is responsible for reviewing the content.
  • One is responsible for coordinating the project.

After one week, the team discovers that:

  • The research is incomplete.
  • The designer has not received the final content.
  • Two team members thought the same person was responsible for reviewing the document.
  • The project coordinator has not updated the timeline.
  • The supervisor has now asked the team to add two additional sections.

Group Task

Your team must decide how to recover the project.

1. What went wrong?
2. Which tasks are now the priority?
3. Who should be responsible for each task?
4. What deadlines should be established?
5. What risks could affect the remaining week?
6. What should be communicated to the supervisor?
7. Which project management tool would help the team?
8. How could the team prevent the same problems from happening again?

Reflection

Which project management tool would be most useful to you during your internship?
What do you normally struggle with when managing multiple tasks?
What will you do differently when you are assigned your next project?
