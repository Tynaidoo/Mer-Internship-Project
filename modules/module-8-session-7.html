Diversity, Equity, Inclusion and Human Rights

Understand diversity, equity, inclusion and equality; recognise unfair discrimination and contribute to a respectful, accessible working environment.

Session 7 · 1 hour

Purpose & learning objectives

Understand diversity, equity, inclusion and equality; recognise unfair discrimination and contribute to a respectful, accessible working environment.

Learning objectives

By the end of this session, you should be able to:

  1. Define diversity, equity, inclusion and equality.
  2. Identify unfair discrimination in workplace situations.
  3. Explain the Constitution and Employment Equity Act's roles.
  4. Recognise bias, exclusion and unequal access to opportunities.
  5. Identify inclusive behaviours supporting collaboration.

Time allocation: 1 hour

SPARK · Opening discussion

Think before you begin

Two interns must give a presentation. One has reliable internet and a laptop; the other shares a computer and has limited connectivity. Does identical treatment ensure equal opportunity? What support might help without lowering standards?

Key concepts and the legal foundation

Diversity means differences in backgrounds, experiences, perspectives and identities. Equality concerns equal rights and fair application of law; equity recognises barriers and the support needed for fair opportunity; inclusion creates conditions for meaningful participation and belonging. Section 9 of the Constitution addresses equality and unfair discrimination, and the Employment Equity Act establishes employment protections and equity measures.

Workplace exclusion and bias

Examples include assumptions about competence based on accent or background, exclusion from meetings without legitimate reason, derogatory comments about disability or religion, inaccessible tools, favouritism and stereotyped task allocation. Not every difference in treatment is automatically unlawful discrimination; context and applicable law matter.

Accessibility and reasonable accommodation

Workplaces should consider unnecessary barriers and appropriate adjustments such as accessible documents, assistive technology and meeting arrangements. Respect colleagues' privacy and avoid intrusive questions about disabilities or personal circumstances.

Everyday inclusive practice

Use respectful language; avoid stereotypes; share information fairly; credit contributions; use accessible documents; invite quieter colleagues to participate; and raise serious discrimination or harassment through appropriate channels. For fair decisions, use clear criteria, seek evidence and challenge disrespect.
