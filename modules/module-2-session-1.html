Module 2 • Session 1

Understanding the Workplace & Professionalism

Explore what professionalism looks like in practice, how organisational expectations differ, and how reliability, ownership, respect and judgement shape workplace trust.

Professionalism • Reliability • Ownership • Workplace Culture

Purpose of the session

To introduce interns to what it means to behave professionally and help them understand that successful workplace behaviour involves more than technical ability. The session aims to establish a clear understanding of workplace expectations, professional standards, accountability, and organisational culture so interns transition seamlessly into corporate environments, translating broad professionalism into observable behaviours.
Time allocation: 45–60 minutes

Learning outcomes

By the end of the session, interns should be able to:

  • Explain professionalism as a combination of competence, reliability, respect, judgement, and accountability.
  • Demonstrate punctuality, ownership, and appropriate workplace boundaries.
  • Apply workplace etiquette to common face-to-face and digital situations.
  • Identify when organisational expectations should be clarified rather than assumed.
Spark

Imagine two interns with identical technical ability. One is consistently reliable, communicates early when something goes wrong, and treats colleagues respectfully. The other does excellent work but is unpredictable. Who would you trust more, and why?

Defining Professionalism, Reliability & Ownership

Professionalism is not simply wearing a formal suit, sounding serious, or never making mistakes. It is the consistent pattern of behaviours that helps others know they can work with you and trust you.

At its core, professionalism means behaving in a way that demonstrates reliability, respect, responsibility, and good judgement in a particular work context.

Competence & Delivery

Apply your skills carefully and deliver work to an appropriate standard.

Interpersonal Respect

Treat colleagues and stakeholders respectfully in your communication and conduct.

Reliability & Ownership

Be dependable, communicate early and take responsibility for your work.

Adaptability & Demeanor

Respond constructively to change, challenges and unfamiliar situations.

Organisational Expectations & Cultural Differences

Workplace norms are not universal. A tech startup, a law firm, a non-profit, and a creative agency operate under vastly different explicit (written) and implicit (unwritten) rules.

Understanding Workplace Nuance

  • Written Rules: Employee handbooks, working hours, formal policies, expense reporting.
  • Unwritten Norms: Communication pace, decision-making hierarchy, email vs. instant messaging preferences, socialising expectations.
  • Adapting to Culture: During your first few weeks, observe how senior leaders communicate, how decisions are documented, and how team members handle conflict.

The table below shows how communication, hierarchy and decisions, and workplace etiquette may be executed differently based on the type of company culture.

DimensionTraditional Corporate / FinancialAgile Tech / Creative Agency
CommunicationFormal emails, structured memos, scheduled syncsSlack/Teams channels, ad-hoc huddles, brief summaries
