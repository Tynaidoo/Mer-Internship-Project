Transition from Academic to Workplace & Meeting New People

Recognise how expectations around responsibility, communication, teamwork and accountability change as you move from an academic environment into the workplace.

Transition • Workplace roles • Professional introductions

Purpose

To help interns recognise the differences between academic and workplace environments and understand how expectations around responsibility, communication, teamwork and accountability change.

Learning Outcomes

By the end of the session, interns will be able to:

  • Identify key differences between university and the workplace.
  • Identify different workplace roles and levels of seniority.
  • Introduce themselves professionally.
  • Demonstrate basic professional communication when meeting colleagues.

From student to professional

At university/college:
You are often primarily responsible for your own learning.

At work:
You are responsible for both your learning and your contribution.

The table below explains the key differences between the academic and workplace environment.

Academic environmentWorkplace environment
Assignments have defined deadlinesPriorities can change
Lecturers provide structured guidanceManagers may expect initiative
Individual gradesTeam and organisational outcomes
You may choose your scheduleWorking hours are expected
Mistakes can affect a markMistakes can affect customers, colleagues or business outcomes
Feedback often comes after assessmentFeedback can happen continuously
Knowledge is often the goalApplying knowledge is the goal

Understanding roles and seniority

A generic and simple organisational structure:

Intern→Junior / Entry-level employee→Experienced professional→Manager→Senior leader

Seniority does not mean that one person is “more important” than another. Different roles carry different responsibilities, levels of decision-making and accountability.

As an intern, you may encounter:

  • Peers — people working at a similar level.
  • Supervisors/managers — people responsible for guiding work.
  • Senior leaders — people responsible for broader organisational decisions.
  • Clients/customers — people receiving the organisation's products or services.
  • Support functions — HR, Finance, IT, Marketing, etc.

Organisational Roles & Core Responsibilities

Connect each role with its corresponding core responsibility.

Part 2: Scenario-Based Applied Practice

Match the role to the real-world scenario they are primarily responsible for resolving.

Scenario 1: A team member cannot log into their corporate email and needs a multi-factor authentication reset.
Scenario 2: An employee has a question regarding their annual leave balance and health insurance coverage.
Scenario 3: A department needs to review its quarterly budget allocation to approve new vendor expenditures.
Scenario 4: Priorities need shifting mid-week due to a sudden project deadline change, requiring task reassignment across the team.
Scenario 5: An individual needs to draft basic research notes for a client presentation while being mentored by a senior team member.

Meeting new people

A professional introduction should be brief and should help start a conversation. University career centres commonly recommend including your name, educational background/current role and relevant interests or experience.

Below is a 30-second professional introduction template that you can use on your first day.

Name + role/study area + what you're doing here + interest/question

Example

“Hi, I'm Sarah. I'm a third-year marketing student and I'm joining the Marketing team as an intern. I'll be supporting the digital marketing project. I'm really interested in learning more about how the team works with customers.”
