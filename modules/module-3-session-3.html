Module 3 • Session 3

AI Limitations, Critical Thinking & Misinformation

Develop critical judgement around AI errors, bias, over-reliance, privacy, intellectual property and synthetic media risks.

AI Limitations • Critical Thinking • HITL • Privacy & IP

Purpose

To develop critical judgement around AI errors, algorithmic bias, automation bias, cognitive offloading, privacy, intellectual property, copyright compliance, and synthetic media risks.
Time allocation: 45–60 minutes

Learning outcomes

By the end of the session, interns should be able to:

  • Identify systemic AI limitations, including algorithmic bias, transparency gaps, and privacy risks.
  • Recognise and mitigate the risks of automation bias, cognitive offloading, and over-reliance.
  • Apply critical-thinking checks before accepting automated or AI-generated recommendations.
  • Evaluate synthetic media, deepfakes, intellectual property rights, copyright compliance, and data privacy.
  • Implement human-in-the-loop (HITL) decision processes.
Spark

If an automated AI system screens job resumes and systematically penalizes certain demographics due to biased training data, who is ethically and legally responsible?

Algorithmic Bias, Automation Bias & Loss of Critical Thinking

Algorithmic Bias

AI models can inherit historical biases present in training data, producing skewed outputs in areas such as hiring, lending or marketing.

Automation Bias

People may over-rely on automated recommendations because they appear objective, confident or efficient.

Cognitive Offloading

Over-reliance on AI for reasoning and writing can reduce deep cognitive engagement and weaken critical thinking.

Hallucinations / Confabulations

AI can generate information that is false, incomplete, invented or internally inconsistent.

Critical thinking means checking whether an output makes sense, verifying evidence, and assessing the consequences if it is wrong.

Activity — Can You Trust the AI Output?

Instructions: Read each workplace situation and identify the main AI-related risk being demonstrated.

Word Bank: Algorithmic Bias | Automation Bias | Cognitive Offloading | Hallucination/Confabulation | Critical Thinking
1. A company uses an AI hiring tool to screen applicants. The tool consistently ranks candidates from certain backgrounds lower because patterns in its historical training data reflected past discriminatory hiring practices.
2. An AI tool recommends that a particular loan application should be rejected. The employee accepts the recommendation without reviewing the applicant's information because they assume the AI must be objective and accurate.
3. An intern uses AI to write every report and answer every question. Over time, they stop trying to analyse problems themselves and simply copy and paste the AI's responses.
4. An AI-generated report states that a company achieved a 40% increase in revenue and provides a reference to a research report. When the intern checks the original source, the statistic cannot be found and appears to have been invented.
5. Before using an AI-generated recommendation, an employee checks whether the information makes sense, verifies important evidence and considers what could happen if the recommendation is wrong.

Privacy, IP, Deepfakes & Synthetic Media

  • Data Privacy & Leaks: Do not paste confidential, personal, or proprietary information into an AI tool unless explicitly permitted.
  • Intellectual Property (IP) & Copyright: AI-generated outputs can raise copyright, ownership and licensing questions, particularly where generated material incorporates or resembles protected material.
  • Synthetic Media & Deepfakes: Voice cloning and video manipulation can create social-engineering and corporate-fraud risks. Visual “tells” alone are unreliable; verify suspicious media through an independent trusted channel.

Loss of critical thinking

AI can make it tempting to follow a simple Ask → Copy → Submit workflow. Instead, use a deliberate review process:

❓ Ask → 🔍 Evaluate → 🤔 Question → ✅ Verify → 🔁 Adapt → 🎯 Decide

This supports Human-in-the-loop (HITL): a process where people actively participate in an automated system, machine-learning model or AI workflow to supervise, correct or approve decisions.

Activity — Human Skills in an AI-Enabled Workplace

AI can produce first drafts and routine outputs, but humans still need to provide judgement, creativity and critical thinking. Review the capabilities below, then identify the capability most important in each scenario.

Critical Thinking

Checking whether AI's information or answer is accurate, logical and supported by evidence.

Communication

Clearly explaining what you need from AI and communicating the final information effectively to others.

Domain Knowledge

Having enough knowledge of a subject to understand, interpret and identify when AI may be wrong.

Creativity

Generating new ideas, alternatives and approaches rather than simply accepting the first AI-generated suggestion.

Judgement

Deciding when AI should be used, when it should not be used, and when human decision-making is necessary.

Digital Literacy

Knowing how to select, use and work effectively with appropriate digital and AI tools.

Ethical Reasoning

Identifying potential risks, biases, consequences, privacy concerns and ethical issues associated with AI use.

Collaboration

Combining AI capabilities with human knowledge, experience and expertise to achieve better outcomes.

Scenario Challenge

Scenario 1

You use AI to generate a summary of a report. The summary looks convincing, but you notice that one of the statistics seems unusual.

Scenario 2

You need AI to help draft an email to a client. You know what you want the email to achieve, so you provide clear instructions and then review the final message before sending it.

Scenario 3

AI provides you with an answer about a technical issue in your industry. You recognise that the answer doesn't match what you learned during your training.

Scenario 4

You are asked to develop ideas for improving your organisation's customer experience. Instead of simply accepting AI's first suggestion, you use it to generate several possibilities and develop your own alternative.

Scenario 5

Your manager asks whether AI should be used to process confidential employee information. Before using an AI tool, you consider privacy, potential risks and whether AI is appropriate for the task.

Scenario 6

You need to complete a task using AI, but there are several tools available. You compare their features and select the tool that is most suitable for the task.

Scenario 7

An AI-generated recommendation appears to favour one group of customers over another. You investigate why this might be happening and consider the potential consequences before using the recommendation.

Scenario 8

You use AI to analyse information, while your colleague uses their industry experience to interpret the findings. Together, you combine the AI output and human expertise to make a better recommendation.

Checklist

  • I am able to identify systemic AI limitations, including algorithmic bias, transparency gaps, and privacy risks.
  • I am able to recognise and mitigate the risks of automation bias, cognitive offloading, and over-reliance.
  • I can apply critical-thinking checks before accepting automated or AI-generated recommendations.
  • I can evaluate synthetic media, deepfakes, intellectual property rights, copyright compliance, and data privacy.
  • I am able to implement human-in-the-loop (HITL) decision processes.

Reflection

Reflect

What is one situation where you think human judgement should always remain central, even when AI could perform the task? Why?
