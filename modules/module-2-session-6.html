Module 2 • Session 6

Learning, Adaptability & Career Development

Build the habits of an independent learner: investigate first, ask strategic questions, evaluate information, use feedback and turn experience into continuous learning.

Independent Learning • Feedback • Information Literacy • Reflection

Purpose

To foster interns as independent, self-directed learners who can ask useful questions, conduct independent research, find and evaluate trustworthy information, actively seek and act on feedback, and turn experience into learning.
Time allocation: 45–60 minutes

Learning outcomes

Interns should be able to:

  • Ask clear, strategic questions after making a reasonable first attempt.
  • Use a repeatable process for finding, evaluating, and utilizing information to solve ambiguous problems.
  • Seek, interpret, and act constructively on feedback.
  • Reflect on experience using structured feedback loops to establish daily learning habits and goals.
Spark

Is there a difference between asking a manager for help immediately versus researching independently first?

Strategic Questioning & Seeking Feedback

The fastest-growing interns are curiosity-driven, but they question strategically. The strategies below build self-reliance, accelerate learning and foster continuous professional growth.

15-Minute Rule

Action: Research or try to solve the problem on your own for 15 minutes before reaching out for help.

Why it matters: Drives self-sufficiency while preventing you from staying stuck for too long.

Strategic Questioning Framework

Action: Structure questions using Goal + Context + Attempt + Obstacle + Specific Question.

Why it matters: Demonstrates ownership of your learning and gives others the exact details needed to help efficiently.

Active Feedback Seeking

Action: Ask for proactive micro-feedback, such as: “What is one thing I could do to make this spreadsheet clearer next time?” instead of waiting for formal reviews.

Why it matters: Accelerates continuous growth and makes feedback normal, low-stakes and actionable.

Receiving Feedback Well

Action: 👂 Listen → 🔍 Clarify → 👁️ Identify what is actionable → 💭 Decide what to change.

Why it matters: Ensures feedback leads to tangible self-improvement without putting you on the defensive.

Activity: Strategic Questioning — Ask for Help Professionally

Instructions: Read each scenario. Apply the 15-Minute Rule first, then write a professional message using Goal + Context + Attempt + Obstacle + Specific Question. Your message should show that you made a genuine effort before seeking assistance.

Scenario 1 — Unfamiliar Software

You have been asked to create a report using software you have never used before. You have spent 15 minutes exploring the software and reading the available instructions, but you are still unsure how to generate the required report.

Scenario 2 — Unclear Instructions

Your manager has asked you to prepare a presentation for a client. You understand the overall goal, but you are unsure which information the client considers most important. You have reviewed previous presentations but still need clarification.

Scenario 3 — Spreadsheet Problem

You are working on a spreadsheet and have tried several ways to fix a formula that is producing an incorrect result. You have checked the formula and compared it with a previous spreadsheet, but you cannot identify the problem.

Scenario 4 — Prioritising Tasks

You have been given three tasks with similar deadlines. You have reviewed what each task requires, but you are unsure which should be prioritised because you don't yet understand their relative importance.

Scenario 5 — Learning from Feedback

Your supervisor has returned your first draft with several comments. You understand most of the feedback, but one of the comments is unclear. You want to make sure you understand it correctly before revising your work.

Reflective Practice & Independent Learning Habits

Learning is strengthened through information literacy. This includes:

  • Finding and evaluating information.
  • Checking source quality: author, evidence, date, purpose and independent corroboration.
  • Checking internal documentation, past archives and knowledge bases before asking standard questions.

Another learning tool is Kolb’s Learning Cycle, which highlights four stages:

1
Concrete Experience
2
Reflective Observation
3
Abstract Conceptualisation
4
Active Experimentation

Activity: Information Literacy & Kolb’s Learning Cycle

Instructions: Complete each sentence by filling in the missing word or phrase.

1.

When evaluating the quality of a source, you should check the author, evidence, date, purpose, and ________.

2.

Before asking a colleague a standard workplace question, you should first check internal documentation, ________, and knowledge bases.

3.

When searching for information, it is important to evaluate whether the source is ________ and trustworthy.

4.

Information that is supported by multiple independent and reliable sources has greater ________.

5.

The ________ stage of Kolb’s Learning Cycle is where you gain direct experience by doing or experiencing something.

6.

After an experience, thinking about what happened, what went well and what could have been improved is called ________.

7.

In Kolb’s Learning Cycle, ________ involves developing ideas, concepts or theories based on what you have learned.

8.

The stage, ________, involves applying what you have learned by trying a new approach.

9.

The four stages of Kolb’s Learning Cycle are Concrete Experience, Reflective Observation, Abstract Conceptualisation and ________.

  • I can reflect on experience using structured feedback loops to establish daily learning habits and goals.
