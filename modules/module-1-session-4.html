Module 1 • Session 4

Company Culture - Values & SMART Goals

Understand how organisational culture and workplace values shape behaviour, and learn how to translate expectations into SMART goals for your internship.

Culture • Values • Behaviour • SMART Goals

Purpose of the Session

Organisational culture is reflected not only in formal policies and stated values, but also in the everyday behaviours, expectations and practices that shape how people work together. The purpose of this session is to help you understand company culture and workplace values and how these influence the way people communicate, collaborate, make decisions and perform their responsibilities. You will also learn how to translate expectations into SMART goals that provide clear direction for your learning and performance during the internship.
Time allocation: 1 hourActivity 1.4.1: Values in ActionActivity 1.4.2: SMART Goal Scenario Challenge

Learning Outcomes

By the end of the session, you will be able to:

  • Define company/organisational culture and explain why it matters in the workplace.
  • Describe how workplace values influence workplace behaviour.
  • Recognise how an intern's behaviour can either support or undermine organisational values.
  • Explain the five components of a SMART goal.
SPARK – What Would You Do?

You are an intern and your supervisor asks you to complete a task by 15:00. At 14:30, you realise that you are not going to finish it on time. You could remain quiet and hope to finish it later, or you could inform your supervisor that you are experiencing a delay. What would you do?

What is Company Culture?

Company culture, also referred to as organisational culture, is the collection of shared assumptions, expectations, norms and ways of working that influence how people behave within an organisation.

Culture can be seen in:

1

Communication

How people communicate with one another and how managers interact with employees.

2

Decisions

How decisions are made and how employees respond to mistakes.

3

Collaboration

How teams work together and how customers and stakeholders are treated.

4

Expectations

How employees approach deadlines, responsibilities, rewards and discouraged behaviour.

Organisational culture develops through shared learning and becomes visible through the values, assumptions and practices that guide behaviour within an organisation.

Understanding SMART Goals

What Is a Goal?

A goal is something a person intends to achieve. In the workplace, goals can help employees focus their effort, monitor progress and understand what successful performance looks like.

Goal-setting research has found that specific and appropriately challenging goals, together with feedback and commitment, can improve performance and motivation. Goals can be used to structure both work performance and learning.

Example of a Weak Goal

“I want to improve my Microsoft Office skills.”

The goal is useful, but it is too broad.

SMART Version

“By the end of my first month, I will improve my Excel skills by completing two workplace-related Excel tasks independently and demonstrating the ability to use at least five relevant Excel functions.”
  • Specific: Excel skills and functions are identified.
  • Measurable: Two tasks and five functions provide measures.
  • Achievable: It is appropriate for an intern's development.
  • Relevant: Excel may support administrative and workplace responsibilities.
  • Time-bound: It must be achieved within the first month.

Important Point

SMART goals should not become a box-ticking exercise. A useful goal should give an intern a clear direction for action and a way to assess progress.

Activity 1.4.2 – SMART Goal Scenario Challenge

Activity

SMART Goal Scenario Challenge

Scenario: Thabo is an intern in the Finance department. During his first few weeks, he notices that he struggles to organise his tasks. He sometimes forgets deadlines and has to ask his supervisor several times what he should prioritise. His supervisor tells him that he needs to become more organised and take greater ownership of his workload.

Thabo writes the following goal: “I want to become better at time management.”

1. Identify the problem with Thabo's goal.

2. Rewrite the goal as a SMART goal.

3. Identify each SMART component in the new goal.

4. Identify one company value that the goal supports.

Application – My Internship SMART Goal

Create one SMART goal for your internship. You can choose an area such as professional communication, Microsoft Office skills, time management, research, teamwork, administration, presentation skills, customer/stakeholder service, project management, technical skills, confidence or industry knowledge.
