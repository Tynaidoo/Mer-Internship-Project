Module 8 · Session 2

Occupational Health and Safety Policies and Workplace Safety

Identify workplace hazards, understand OHS duties and practise safe responses to incidents.

OHS • Risk Prevention • Incident Reporting

Purpose of the Session

Develop the knowledge and practical skills to recognise workplace hazards, understand basic Occupational Health and Safety (OHS) requirements and respond appropriately to unsafe situations. Explore employer and employee duties under South Africa’s Occupational Health and Safety Act 85 of 1993, workplace safety procedures and risks in physical and digital environments.

The session promotes a proactive safety culture based on hazard recognition, established procedures, incident reporting and prevention of workplace injury and ill health.

Learning Outcomes

  • Explain the purpose of OHS legislation and workplace safety policies.
  • Identify physical, ergonomic, environmental and psychosocial hazards.
  • Describe employer and employee responsibilities under the OHS Act.
  • Distinguish a hazard, risk, incident and near miss.
  • Respond appropriately to unsafe conditions and emergencies.

Time allocation: 1 hour

SPARK — Spot the danger

At the office entrance you notice a wet floor, cables across a walkway, boxes blocking an exit and a colleague lifting a heavy box with poor posture. How many hazards can you identify? Which needs immediate attention? What could happen if no one reports them?

Understanding Occupational Health and Safety

OHS prevents work-related injury and ill health through identifying hazards, assessing risks and implementing suitable controls. South Africa’s Occupational Health and Safety Act 85 of 1993 sets important duties for the safety of workers and others affected by workplace activities. Organisational OHS policies explain reporting, equipment use, emergency response and safety requirements.

Key OHS concepts

ConceptMeaningExample
HazardSomething with potential to cause harmAn exposed electrical wire
RiskLikelihood and consequences of harmSomeone could contact the wire
IncidentEvent that causes or could cause harmA worker slips on a wet floor
Near missEvent that could have caused harm but did notSomeone trips over a cable without injury
Control measureAction eliminating or reducing riskSafely removing a cable from a walkway

Employer and Employee Responsibilities

Employer responsibilities

Section 8 of the OHS Act establishes a general duty, as far as reasonably practicable, to provide and maintain a working environment safe and without risk to employees’ health. Relevant responsibilities include hazard identification and controls, training, instruction, supervision, safe equipment and reporting procedures. Precise legal duties depend on the circumstances and applicable regulations.

Employee responsibilities

Section 14 includes taking reasonable care of one’s own and others’ safety, cooperating with the employer and reporting specified unsafe or unhealthy situations. Interns should follow instructions, use equipment properly, report hazards and near misses, avoid unnecessary risks and ask for guidance when unsure.

Activity 8.2.1 — Workplace Hazard Hunt

Apply hazard-identification principles to workplace situations.

  • Office: A cable crosses a walkway, boxes obstruct an emergency exit and a spill remains unmarked.
  • Workstation: A poorly positioned screen, uncomfortable chair and few opportunities to change posture.
  • Workshop: A machine has a missing protective guard and a colleague suggests using it briefly.

Identify the highest-priority hazard, whom to notify and appropriate precautions. Do not attempt repairs or handle equipment beyond your training or authority.

Risk Prevention, Incident Reporting & Emergency Response

The risk-management process

Preventing hazards

Prioritise eliminating hazards where reasonably practicable. Controls may include removal, safer substitutions, physical safeguards, safe procedures, training and supervision, and appropriate PPE. PPE should not automatically be the first or only solution.

Reporting incidents and near misses

Near misses can reveal safety weaknesses even without injury. Know who receives reports, how to report urgent hazards, where forms are located, how to provide accurate information and how to follow up unresolved hazards. Applicable legal reporting requirements vary.

Emergency response

  1. Raise the alarm and contact designated responders.
  2. Follow the workplace evacuation or emergency plan.
  3. Avoid dangerous areas or tasks beyond your training.
  4. Assist only within your training without creating further danger.
  5. Report the incident appropriately once safe.

Emergency routes, assembly points, first-aid arrangements and contact numbers must be specific to the organisation and premises.

Activity 8.2.2 — Safety First: Respond to the Incident

15 minutes | Team simulation and decision-making. Practise prioritising safety and communicating hazards.

The scenario

During an internship event, a participant slips on a wet floor near an electrical extension cable, falls and reports pain in their wrist. People gather and a colleague attempts to move the cable while equipment is still connected.

Emergency response challenge

  1. What is the immediate priority: continue the event, protect people and arrange appropriate assistance, or move all equipment regardless of electrical risk?
  2. What should happen to the cable: let anyone disconnect it, ignore it, or keep people away while an authorised competent person manages it?
  3. What should happen afterwards: resume without recording, document and report accurately, or blame a colleague?

Team simulation

Assign a safety observer, incident reporter, communicator and team spokesperson. Allow five minutes to prepare and five minutes to present and receive feedback. Clarify the difference between emergency response, incident reporting and subsequent prevention. This learning simulation does not replace site-specific emergency training.

Checklist & Reflection

  • I can explain OHS legislation and workplace policies.
  • I can identify physical, ergonomic, environmental and psychosocial hazards.
  • I understand employer and employee duties under the OHS Act.
  • I can distinguish a hazard, risk, incident and near miss.
  • I can respond appropriately to unsafe conditions and emergencies.

Reflection

  1. What hazard might I encounter and how can I reduce the risk?
  2. What if I noticed a serious concern but felt uncomfortable reporting it?
  3. Why report near misses when nobody is injured?
