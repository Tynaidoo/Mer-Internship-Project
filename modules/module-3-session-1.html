Module 3 • Session 1

Introduction to AI & Generative AI

A practical foundation in artificial intelligence, generative AI, workplace use, prompt engineering and responsible human oversight.

AI • Generative AI • Prompt Engineering • Human-in-the-Loop

Purpose

Provide interns with a practical foundation in AI and generative AI, including capabilities, limitations, workplace uses, prompt engineering techniques, and verification/human oversight boundaries.
Time allocation: 45–60 minutes

Learning outcomes

By the end of the session, interns should be able to:

  • Explain and distinguish between traditional Machine Learning and Generative AI technologies.
  • Identify appropriate and inappropriate workplace uses, strengths, and fundamental boundaries of AI tools.
  • Write highly useful prompts utilising structured prompt engineering models such as CLEAR.
  • Apply human-in-the-loop (HITL) verification and judgment to AI outputs before professional use.

Unpacking Artificial Intelligence

Artificial intelligence (AI) refers to computer systems designed to perform tasks that typically require aspects of human intelligence, such as recognising patterns, making predictions, understanding language or generating content.

The AI Ecosystem: Key Concepts

Terms such as AI, ML, deep learning, LLM and generative AI are often used interchangeably. They are related, but they are not the same thing.

ConceptWhat does it mean?FunctionsLimitationsExamples
AIThe broad field of creating systems capable of performing tasks associated with intelligence.Solve problems, recognise patterns, make predictions, understand language, perceive images, automate decisions.It does not necessarily possess human understanding, consciousness or common sense.Recommendation systems, fraud detection, autonomous vehicles, virtual assistants.
Machine Learning (ML)A branch of AI in which systems learn patterns from data rather than being explicitly programmed for every situation.Predict outcomes, classify information, identify patterns, detect anomalies and make recommendations.Depends heavily on quality and relevance of training data and may perform poorly on unfamiliar situations.Spam filters, credit-risk models, Netflix recommendations, fraud detection.
Deep LearningA type of ML using multi-layered neural networks to learn complex patterns.Handle complex tasks involving images, audio, language and other high-dimensional data.Can require large amounts of data and computing power; internal reasoning can be difficult to interpret.Image recognition, speech recognition, modern language models.
Natural Language Processing (NLP)AI techniques for working with human language.Analyse, translate, classify, summarise and generate text.Language can be ambiguous, culturally dependent and context-sensitive.Translation systems, sentiment analysis, chatbots.
Large Language Model (LLM)A large AI model trained on enormous amounts of text and other data to process and generate language.Generate and transform text, answer questions, summarise, brainstorm, translate, write and analyse information.Can produce convincing but incorrect information (“hallucinations”) and does not automatically know whether an answer is true.GPT models, Claude, Gemini, Llama.
Generative AI (GenAI)AI designed to generate new content based on patterns learned from data.Generate text, images, audio, video, code and other content.Generated content can be inaccurate, biased, repetitive, inappropriate or factually fabricated.ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Midjourney, DALL-E.
Multimodal AIAI that can work with multiple types of information, such as text, images, audio and video.Analyse an image and text together, discuss documents, interpret charts, process speech.Performance can vary significantly depending on modality and task.ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude.
AI Agent / Agentic AIAn AI system designed to pursue a goal by planning and taking multiple actions, often using tools or software.Perform multi-step tasks, retrieve information, use tools, interact with software and automate workflows.Agents can make incorrect decisions, take unintended actions or amplify mistakes across multiple steps. Human oversight is particularly important.Coding agents, research agents, workflow automation agents.

Prompt Engineering, Verification & Oversight

AI outputs are only as good as the input prompts. A useful prompt includes role/context, task, relevant information, constraints, desired format, and quality criteria.

The CLEAR framework, created by Dr. Leo Lo of the University of New Mexico, encapsulates five main components of prompt creation:

Human in the Loop

AI is a drafting tool; human verification is required to check calculations, verify claims against sources, check tone, and protect confidentiality.

Before using AI at work, ask yourself:

Is AI appropriate for this task?

Not every problem needs AI.

What information am I giving the AI?

Could the information be confidential, personal or sensitive?

How important is the decision?

The higher the stakes, the greater the need for human review.

How will I verify the output?

Can I check the facts against reliable sources?

Who is accountable for the final result?

Ultimately, the human using the AI remains responsible for how the output is used.

Activity: Prompt Engineering Optimisation Challenge

  1. Take this (or your own) weak prompt: “Write an email about project delays.”
  2. Upgrade it using the CLEAR framework.
  3. Run both the weak and CLEAR prompts in an AI tool of your choice.
  4. Submit your upgraded CLEAR prompt and the resulting AI response.
