Module 3 • Session 4

Cybersecurity & Data Privacy

Protect workplace information, recognise social engineering and phishing, and build safe digital habits in hybrid environments.

Cybersecurity • Data Privacy • Phishing • Digital Hygiene

Purpose

To educate interns on data classification, practical habits for protecting information, recognising social engineering, corporate privacy policies, and digital hygiene in hybrid environments.
Time allocation: 45–60 minutes

Learning outcomes

By the end of the session, interns should be able to:

  • Distinguish and categorise organisational data types (Personal, Sensitive, Proprietary, Public).
  • Explain why access permissions, least privilege, and security controls matter.
  • Recognise and report social engineering and phishing threat vectors.
  • Apply safe digital practices to links, attachments, public Wi-Fi, and data sharing.
  • Respond immediately and appropriately when a security incident or leak is suspected.
Spark

Imagine you accidentally send a confidential company document to the wrong person. What should you do first?

Data Classification, Privacy & Free Service Risks

Data is information that can be collected, stored, processed or shared. Poor data protection can cause financial loss, fraud, privacy violations, reputational damage, legal or regulatory consequences, and loss of customer trust.

DataExample
Public DataPress releases, public marketing collateral, blog drafts
Internal DataOrganisational charts, internal news, training materials
Confidential / Sensitive DataFinancial reports, product roadmaps, business plans, trade secrets
Personally Identifiable Information (PII) / Restricted DataID numbers, home addresses, banking details, customer records and salary spreadsheets
The “Free Service” Trade-off: Free tools such as PDF converters, free VPNs and grammar checkers may collect or monetise uploaded data. Avoid using unapproved tools for proprietary or customer data.

The intern's data rule

If you don't need access to it, don't access it.
If you don't need to share it, don't share it.
If you're unsure, ask.

Phishing, Social Engineering & Incident Response

  • Spear Phishing: Highly targeted messages impersonating managers or vendors and requesting urgent payments or credentials.
  • Social Engineering: Exploits human trust, urgency, fear, authority or helpfulness.
  • Red Flags: Sender mismatch, artificial urgency, generic greetings, suspicious links, unexpected attachments or requests to bypass normal processes.
  • Public Wi-Fi Risks: Untrusted networks can expose data to interception. Follow your organisation's approved secure-access procedures and lock your screen when stepping away.

Immediate Incident Response

Early reporting can limit harm. If you click a suspicious link, expose information or lose a device:

⛔ Stop further exposure → 📝 Preserve information → 📞 Contact the approved security channel immediately

Activity — Spot the Phish

Instructions: Read each workplace message. Identify whether it is safe or suspicious, identify the red flags, and decide what you should do.

Scenario 1 — Account Warning

From: IT Support <it-support@company-securehelp.com>
Subject: URGENT: Your account will be disabled today

Your account has been flagged for suspicious activity. You must verify your password immediately by clicking the link below. Failure to do so within 30 minutes will result in your account being disabled.

Scenario 2 — Urgent Payment

From: Your Manager
Subject: Urgent payment

Hi, I am in a meeting and need you to urgently purchase gift cards for a client. Please send me the codes as soon as possible. Don't call me as I can't take calls right now.

Scenario 3 — Unexpected Attachment

From: A colleague you regularly work with
Subject: Meeting notes

Hi, Please find the meeting notes attached. These are the notes from today's team meeting. Thanks.

You were not expecting an attachment and the colleague has not mentioned sending you a document.

Scenario 4 — Public Wi-Fi

You are working from a coffee shop and connect to a network called “Free_Coffee_WiFi” without checking whether it is the official network.

A. Your computer will automatically be damaged.
B. Your data could potentially be intercepted on an insecure network.
C. Your company account will automatically be deleted.
D. There is no risk if the Wi-Fi has a password.

Scenario 5 — Incident Response

You accidentally click a suspicious link and realise afterwards that it may have been a phishing attempt. What should you do first?

A. Ignore it if nothing immediately appears to happen.
B. Delete the email and continue working.
C. Stop further exposure, preserve relevant information and immediately contact the approved security channel.
D. Forward the email to your colleagues to warn them.
