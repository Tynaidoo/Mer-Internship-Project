Module 7 · Session 2

Managing Presentation Anxiety and Delivering Effective Presentations

Manage presentation anxiety and strengthen confident, professional delivery through preparation, rehearsal, voice, body language, eye contact and audience engagement.

Preparation • Confidence • Delivery • Audience Engagement

Purpose of Session

This session helps you manage presentation anxiety and deliver presentations confidently and professionally. You will learn practical strategies for preparation, rehearsal and effective delivery, including the use of voice, body language, eye contact and audience engagement.

Learning Outcomes

  • Identify common causes and signs of presentation anxiety.
  • Apply practical strategies for managing presentation anxiety.
  • Explain the importance of preparation and rehearsal.
  • Use voice, pace, pauses and emphasis effectively.
  • Apply appropriate body language and eye contact.
  • Engage professionally with an audience.
  • Respond appropriately to audience questions.
Spark

What Happens When You Have to Present?

Imagine your supervisor asks you to give the team a five-minute presentation tomorrow. What is your immediate reaction?

What Is Presentation Anxiety?

Presentation anxiety can arise from uncertainty about the audience, your preparation, your knowledge of the topic, possible mistakes or unexpected questions. Recognising what triggers your anxiety can help you distinguish what you can control from what you can prepare for.

Activity 1.1

What Makes You Nervous?

Rank these from 1 – Least anxiety to 6 – Most anxiety: presenting to two familiar colleagues; presenting to your supervisor; presenting to 30 strangers; presenting a topic you know extremely well; presenting without enough preparation; answering unexpected questions.

  1. What makes some situations more stressful?
  2. Is the anxiety caused by the audience, preparation, topic knowledge or something else?
  3. Which factors can you control?
  4. Which factors can you prepare for?

Preparation Reduces Uncertainty

Preparation creates familiarity. Before presenting, understand your topic, audience, purpose, main message, structure, available time and what you want the audience to remember. Understand your material rather than memorising every sentence.

Know Your Opening

Prepare how you will greet the audience, introduce yourself if necessary, deliver your first sentence, state the purpose and introduce the topic. Knowing how you will begin can remove uncertainty from the first moments.

Rehearse — Don't Just Read Your Slides

Practise aloud, time yourself and rehearse difficult sections so that delivering the presentation becomes more familiar.

Activity 1.2

Preparing to Present

You have a five-minute presentation tomorrow and 30 minutes available this evening. Develop a preparation plan. Prioritise five actions from reviewing content, checking the key message, practising the opening, rehearsing aloud, timing yourself, practising difficult sections, anticipating questions and checking technology.

Managing Presentation Anxiety

There is no single strategy that works for everyone. Useful approaches include practice and exposure as well as changing unhelpful thinking patterns.

Prepare Early

Give yourself time to understand and practise your material.

Practise Aloud

Repeated delivery makes the situation less unfamiliar.

Know Your Material

Confidence is easier when you understand what you are talking about.

Focus on Your Message

Redirect attention from “What does everyone think about me?” towards “What does my audience need to understand?”

Avoid Expecting Perfection

Pausing, correcting yourself, looking at notes or receiving a difficult question does not automatically make a presentation unsuccessful.

Pause

If you lose your place, stop briefly, find your next point and continue.

How You Say It Matters

VOICE + BODY LANGUAGE + EYE CONTACT + ENGAGEMENT

Using Your Voice

Volume: Speak loudly enough for the audience and environment.
Pace: Slow down deliberately if anxiety makes you rush.
Pauses: Use silence after important statements, before new ideas, after questions or when gathering your thoughts.
Emphasis: Change your voice to highlight important ideas.

Activity 1.3

How You Say It Matters

Read: “Our research identified three important opportunities for improving the current process.”

First read it quickly without pausing. Then repeat it more slowly, emphasise “three important opportunities,” pause after “research,” and look up at your audience.

Which version sounded more confident and made the key message easier to identify?

Body Language

Consider posture, facial expression, gestures, movement and where you direct your attention. Aim for natural, purposeful body language.

Eye Contact

Periodically look at different people or areas of the audience rather than only at slides, notes, the floor or one person. Online, looking at the camera can help create the impression of eye contact.

Your Slides Are Not Your Script

Avoid reading every word from the screen. Your role is to explain, interpret and communicate information while slides support audience understanding.

What If You Make a Mistake?

  1. Pause.
  2. Correct the information if necessary.
  3. Continue.

Avoid repeatedly apologising for small mistakes; the audience may not even have noticed.

Handling Questions

  1. Listen — allow the person to finish.
  2. Pause — take a moment to think.
  3. Clarify — ask for clarification if necessary.
  4. Answer clearly — respond directly before adding detail.
  5. Be honest — if you do not know, say so and offer to check and follow up rather than inventing an answer.

Before & During Your Presentation

Before You Present

  • Arrive early where possible.
  • Check the room or online meeting.
  • Test slides, audio and video.
  • Check videos and links.
  • Keep a backup copy.
  • Know how much time you have.
  • Take a moment to settle before beginning.

During Your Presentation

BREATHE • LOOK UP • SLOW DOWN • PAUSE • COMMUNICATE THE MESSAGE

You do not need to rush, memorise every sentence or be perfect. Your goal is to help your audience understand your message.

Activity 1.4

Deliver and Receive Feedback

Choose one slide from Session 1 and present it to another intern for 60–90 seconds. Your partner should observe voice, body language, eye contact, message clarity and overall delivery. Identify one strength and one improvement, switch roles, then present your slide a second time while applying the feedback.
