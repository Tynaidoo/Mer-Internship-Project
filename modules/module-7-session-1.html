Module 7 · Session 1

Designing Effective Presentations

Plan and design professional presentations that communicate a clear message, use relevant evidence and help audiences understand information.

Audience • Message • Structure • Slide Design

Purpose of Session

This session helps you understand how to plan and design an effective professional presentation. During your internship, you may present project updates, research findings, ideas, recommendations or completed work to colleagues, supervisors or other stakeholders. An effective presentation should communicate a clear message, provide relevant supporting evidence and help the audience understand the information being presented.

Learning Outcomes

  • Explain the purpose of an effective presentation.
  • Identify the audience, purpose and key message.
  • Structure a presentation logically.
  • Apply basic principles of effective slide design.
  • Use text, images, graphs and other visual evidence appropriately.
  • Identify and improve ineffective presentation slides.
Spark

Think About a Presentation You Remember

Think of a presentation you found particularly effective or ineffective. What made you pay attention? Was the message easy to understand? Were the slides easy to follow? What do you still remember?

What makes a presentation effective from the audience's perspective?

What Is an Effective Presentation?

An effective presentation is more than a collection of attractive slides. It begins with a clear purpose and audience, communicates a focused message, uses relevant evidence and organises information so the audience can follow it.

Start With Your Audience

Before designing slides, consider who will receive the message, what they already know, what they need to understand and what you want them to think, know or do after the presentation.

Activity 1.1

What Makes a Presentation Effective?

Compare Presentation A—long paragraphs, small text, many bullets, complicated graphs and inconsistent fonts—with Presentation B—clear messages, relevant visuals, readable text, simple graphs and consistent design.

  1. Which would be easier to follow, and why?
  2. What problems could Presentation A create?
  3. Identify three principles you would use in your own presentation.

Begin With the Message, Not the Slides

Clarify your audience, purpose, key message and desired audience response before opening presentation software.

Activity 1.2

Identify Your Key Message

Imagine you have five minutes to present an internship project. Identify your audience, the purpose, the most important thing the audience should understand and what you want them to do afterwards.

Structuring Your Presentation

Organise information logically so each part contributes to your central message. A useful test is: If my audience remembers only one thing from my presentation, what should it be?

Designing Your Slides

Slides support your communication; they should not contain everything you plan to say. Remove material that does not help the audience understand the message.

One Clear Message at a Time

Where possible, each slide should communicate one main idea. The Assertion-Evidence approach can be summarised as:

ASSERTION → EVIDENCE

Assertion: What do I want the audience to understand?
Evidence: What can I show that supports the message?

Evidence may include a graph, image, diagram, quotation, statistic, process or comparison.

Text: Less Can Be More

Avoid turning a slide into a document. Use a clear message and supporting evidence, while explaining detail verbally.

Use Visuals With a Purpose

Photographs, diagrams, graphs, charts, timelines, icons and illustrations should help communicate information rather than merely decorate the slide.

Presenting Data, Consistency & Readability

Graphs should communicate the main finding, use clear labels, remove unnecessary information and make the important information easy to identify. Tell the audience what they should notice.

Consistency Matters

Use consistent fonts, headings, alignment, colours, image styles, spacing, layouts and terminology so the presentation feels cohesive and professional.

Readability

  • Use text large enough to read from a distance.
  • Maintain sufficient contrast.
  • Avoid overcrowding.
  • Make the main message identifiable quickly.
  • Use adequate spacing.
  • Ensure graph labels are readable.
Activity 1.3

Fix the Slide

A project-update slide contains a long background paragraph plus six generic findings about communication, slow processes, training needs, inconsistent processes, difficult-to-find information and opportunities for improvement.

  1. What is wrong with the slide?
  2. What is the most important message?
  3. What could be removed?
  4. What should be explained verbally?
  5. What visual could replace some text?

Redesign it as: One clear message + supporting evidence.

Common Presentation Design Mistakes

Watch for too much text, reading directly from slides, too many bullets, small fonts, unnecessary animations, complicated graphs, irrelevant images, inconsistent layouts, too many ideas, unclear headings and excessive information.

The goal is not to make slides as simple as possible. The goal is to make the message as clear as possible.

Activity 1.4

Design an Effective Presentation Slide

Choose research, a project, task, problem, learning experience or recommendation from your internship. Create one slide containing a clear message, evidence and a simple professional design. Show it to another intern and ask: “What is the main message of my slide?”

Checklist

  • ✓ I can identify the purpose and audience of my presentation.
  • ✓ I can define its main message and logical structure.
  • ✓ My slides communicate clear messages.
  • ✓ I use visuals to support information rather than decorate slides.
  • ✓ I avoid unnecessary text.
  • ✓ My graphs and visual information are easy to understand.
  • ✓ My presentation uses consistent formatting and readable slides.
  • ✓ I understand the difference between a topic heading and a message.
  • ✓ My slides support what I say rather than replacing me.

Reflection

Complete: “The biggest change I will make when designing my next presentation is...” Then identify one presentation habit you will stop, one design principle you will start using and one thing you will check before presenting. Add your reflection to your Internship ePortfolio.
