Wage Regulation and Fair Remuneration

Understanding wage regulation

The National Minimum Wage Act 9 of 2018 establishes minimum wage requirements; the Basic Conditions of Employment Act 75 of 1997 (BCEA) regulates remuneration records, deductions, hours and leave; and the Labour Relations Act 66 of 1995 governs labour relations and dispute resolution. From 1 March 2026, the national minimum wage is R30.23 per ordinary hour worked, subject to the applicable legal arrangements. An internship, learnership and bursary-linked placement are not automatically the same legal arrangement. A job title alone does not determine legal rights.

Payslips and deductions

Where the BCEA provisions apply, payslips should provide particulars such as the employer, employee, payment period, remuneration and deductions. Gross pay is remuneration before deductions; net pay is the amount received afterwards. Check recorded hours, agreed rates, overtime where applicable, UIF or tax contributions and unexplained deductions. Not every deduction is unlawful, but its legal basis should be established.

Responding to wage problems

Compare the payslip with the agreement and hours worked. Request a written explanation from payroll or the supervisor, preserve the records and escalate unresolved concerns to HR or the appropriate workplace representative. Seek advice from the Department of Employment and Labour or an appropriate dispute-resolution body.
