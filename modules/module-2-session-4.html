Growth Mindset, Grit & Resilience

Develop practical ways to respond to workplace challenges, setbacks, constructive criticism and steep learning curves with adaptability, persistence and reflection.

Growth Mindset • Grit • Resilience • Productive Persistence

Purpose

Foster psychological resilience, adaptability, and a growth mindset in interns when facing workplace challenges, setbacks, constructive criticism, and steep learning curves.
Time allocation: 45–60 minutes

Learning outcomes

Interns should be able to:

  • Differentiate between Fixed and Growth Mindsets in workplace learning environments.
  • Distinguish a growth-oriented response from fixed thinking.
  • Apply Angela Duckworth’s Grit framework and practical strategies for persistence, recovery, and problem-solving.
  • Reframe setbacks as information while avoiding unrealistic “just try harder” messages.
Spark

Think about a time you received critical feedback or failed at a task. What was your immediate internal dialogue, and how did it affect your next attempt?

Fixed vs. Growth Mindset at Work

Pioneered by Carol Dweck, a Growth Mindset is the belief that abilities can be developed through dedication, practice, feedback, and support.

Fixed Mindset Traps

  • Viewing feedback as personal criticism.
  • Avoiding difficult tasks for fear of failure.
  • Giving up quickly.
  • Thinking: “I'm just not good at presentations.”

Growth Mindset Behaviours

  • Embracing challenges.
  • Viewing effort as the path to mastery.
  • Learning from feedback.
  • Finding inspiration in others' success.

The Power of “Yet”

Replace “I don't know how to analyse this data” with “I don't know how to analyse this data YET.” Setbacks are treated as information, not a verdict on your ability.

Activity 1 — Self-Directed Mindset Reframing Audit

Identify 2 areas in your professional or academic life where you catch yourself using “Fixed Mindset” thinking. Rewrite those statements into explicit Growth Mindset action plans using:

Fixed Statement → The “Yet” Pivot → Concrete Action Step

Deliverable: Post two–four transformed Growth Statements on the discussion.

Developing Grit & Workplace Resilience

Grit—defined by Angela Duckworth as passion and perseverance for long-term goals—is a key predictor of career success. Workplace resilience is the ability to recover quickly from difficulties, setbacks, and high-pressure situations.

Resilience Strategies for Interns

  1. Reframing Setbacks: Viewing mistakes as data points rather than personal failures.
  2. Emotional Regulation: Pausing before reacting to negative news or tough feedback.
  3. Seeking Tactical Support: Asking for guidance early rather than suffering in silence.

Productive Persistence

Persistence is useful when paired with learning. A productive loop is:

📝 Attempt → 🧐 Observe → 🗣️ Seek Feedback → 🛠️ Adjust Strategy → 🔁 Retry

Interns should also distinguish controllable from uncontrollable factors. If a deadline changes because a client cancels, resilience may mean adapting the plan rather than simply working longer.

Activity: Turning Setbacks into Growth

Part 1 — Productive Persistence

Scenario: You are an intern who has spent two days preparing a presentation. During your first review, your supervisor points out several problems and asks you to make significant changes before presenting it to the client.

Complete the five steps:

StepYour response
📝 Attempt – What did you originally try to do?
🧐 Observe – What can you learn from the feedback or setback?
🗣️ Seek feedback – What question could you ask your supervisor?
🛠️ Adjust strategy – What would you change?
🔁 Retry – What would you do differently when trying again?
Part 2 — Resilience Check

Which response demonstrates the strongest workplace resilience?

Part 3 — In My Control or Not?

Based on Parts 1 and 2, identify which parts of this situation you can control and which you cannot control.
