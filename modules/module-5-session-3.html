Module 5 · Session 3

Feedback

Learn to give, receive and use specific, respectful and constructive feedback for workplace learning and performance improvement.

Feedback • Professional Growth • Peer Learning

Purpose

This session helps interns understand the importance of feedback and develop the skills needed to give, receive and use feedback effectively in the workplace. Feedback supports learning, professional development and performance improvement by showing what works, what can improve and what action can be taken next.

Learning Outcomes

  • Define feedback and explain its workplace purpose.
  • Explain its importance for learning, performance and professional development.
  • Respond professionally and effectively to feedback.
  • Describe strategies for giving peer feedback.
  • Give specific, respectful and constructive peer feedback.
Spark

How Does Feedback Make You Feel?

Think about feedback you have received. What was it about? How did it make you feel? What made it helpful or unhelpful? Did you change anything afterwards? What could have made it more useful?

What Is Feedback?

Feedback is information about a person's performance, behaviour, work or progress that can help maintain effective performance or improve future performance. It may come formally or informally from supervisors, mentors, colleagues, clients or peers.

  • Reinforce what has been done well.
  • Identify areas for improvement.
  • Clarify expectations and correct errors.
  • Encourage learning and improve future performance.
  • Support professional development.
  • Show how work or behaviour affects others.
Remember

Feedback is not only criticism. Positive and developmental feedback both contribute to professional learning.

Activity 5.3.1

Feedback Detective

Classify each statement as Constructive, Unhelpful or Needs Improvement, then explain your decision.

Statement 1

“You did a great job.”

Statement 2

“You are not very organised.”

Statement 3

“The introduction clearly explained the purpose of the presentation. One area you could improve is the transition between sections. Adding a short linking statement could make the presentation easier to follow.”

Statement 4

“You need to work harder.”

Statement 5

“You interrupted the discussion several times. Next time, try allowing the speaker to finish before responding so that everyone’s ideas can be heard.”

Statement 6

“That report was terrible.”

Asking for Feedback

Effective interns actively seek feedback. Specific questions make useful responses easier.

  • What is one thing I did well?
  • What is one thing I could improve?
  • Was my explanation clear?
  • How could I improve this before presenting it to the client?
  • What should I do differently next time?
  • What should I focus on improving over the next month?

Responding to Feedback Effectively

Listen → Clarify → Identify what is actionable → Decide what to change

AccuracyIs the information correct and relevant?

CommunicationIs it appropriate for the intended audience?
Team contributionDid the person contribute meaningfully?
ProfessionalismDoes it meet workplace standards?
ImprovementWhat one specific thing could make it stronger?
Activity 5.3.2

The Feedback Conversation

You are working on a team presentation with another intern, Lebo. Lebo's slides are accurate and well researched, but contain large blocks of text. During practice, Lebo reads directly from the slides and rarely looks at the audience. Your supervisor asks you to provide peer feedback before the final presentation.

1. What did Lebo do well?

2. What specific behaviour could be improved?

3. How would you communicate the feedback respectfully?

4. What practical suggestion could you give Lebo?

5. How could you encourage Lebo to respond constructively to the feedback?

Checklist & Reflection

  • I can respond professionally and effectively to feedback.
  • I can describe strategies for giving peer feedback.
  • I can give specific, respectful and constructive peer feedback.

Reflection

How could regularly asking for feedback contribute to your professional development?
