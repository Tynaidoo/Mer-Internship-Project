Module 4 • Session 2

Time Management

Understand how you use your time, balance work and study responsibilities, and apply practical tools to manage competing priorities.

Time Management • Work–Study Balance • Self-Regulation

Purpose of Session

The purpose of this session is to help you understand time management, help you balance a healthy work-study life and introduce you to time management tools. As an intern, you are likely to manage multiple responsibilities at the same time, including internship tasks, learning activities, deadlines, meetings and personal commitments. Developing an awareness of how you currently use your time is an important first step towards managing your responsibilities effectively.

Learning Outcomes

By the end of this session, you should be able to:

  • Define time management and explain its importance.
  • Identify your own time-management tendencies and areas for improvement.
  • Balance a healthy work-study life.
  • Apply time-management tools.
Time allocation: 1 hour

What Is Time Management?

Time management is the process of planning and organising how you use your available time to accomplish tasks and goals.

Effective time management is not about doing more things. It is about making intentional decisions about what to do, when to do it, and how it fits with your other responsibilities.

For interns, this includes balancing:

  • internship responsibilities
  • learning and study tasks
  • deadlines and projects
  • meetings and appointments
  • personal responsibilities
  • rest and wellbeing

Time management is closely linked to self-regulated learning, where individuals actively plan, monitor and adjust their behaviour to achieve goals.

Why Does Time Management Matter?

Time is a limited resource. Everyone has the same number of hours in a day, but how those hours are used makes a significant difference.

Effective time management helps you to:

  • meet deadlines
  • manage competing responsibilities
  • reduce last-minute stress
  • stay focused on important tasks
  • make consistent progress
  • create time for learning and development
  • manage distractions
  • communicate effectively
  • maintain personal wellbeing

Good Time Management and Poor Time Management

Effective Time ManagementIneffective Time Management
Planning aheadForgetting deadlines
Prioritising tasksTreating all tasks as equal
Starting earlyLeaving tasks to the last minute
Estimating time realisticallyUnderestimating task duration
Tracking progressLosing track of tasks
Managing distractionsFrequent task switching
Communicating needsMissing deadlines silently
Taking breaksWorking without rest
Adjusting plansBecoming overwhelmed easily

Activity 4.2.2: Importance of Work-Study Life Balance

Scenario

Lerato is an intern who is also completing part-time studies. Recently, she has been assigned a demanding project at work. At the same time, she has an upcoming assignment, family responsibilities, and a personal commitment to a community organisation.

Over the past two weeks, Lerato has been working late every night. She has stopped exercising, rarely sees her friends, and often feels tired during the day. Although she is completing her tasks, she feels stressed and overwhelmed.

1. Identify the problem

What signs show that Lerato’s time management is unbalanced?

2. Prioritisation

What could be adjusted or delayed?
