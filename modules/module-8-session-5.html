Legal Literacy and Employment Contracts

Introduce the main laws governing employment and workplace training in South Africa so interns can understand agreements, recognise responsibilities and seek guidance about unclear terms.

Session 5 · 1 hour

Purpose & learning objectives

Introduce the main laws governing employment and workplace training in South Africa so interns can understand agreements, recognise responsibilities and seek guidance about unclear terms.

Learning objectives

By the end of this session, you should be able to:

  1. Identify the purpose of the main South African employment laws.
  2. Locate and interpret key terms in an internship agreement.
  3. Distinguish internships, learnerships, fixed-term employment and independent contracting.
  4. Identify workplace rights and responsibilities.
  5. Understand why written documentation and clarification matter.

Time allocation: 1 hour

SPARK · Opening discussion

Think before you begin

An internship offer states only the stipend and start date. It says nothing about hours, leave, supervision, confidentiality or end date. Write three questions to ask, two risks of relying on verbal promises and one source of assistance.

The Constitution and fair labour practices

The Constitution protects the right to fair labour practices. Relevant legislation includes the Labour Relations Act, BCEA, National Minimum Wage Act, Employment Equity Act, Occupational Health and Safety Act and POPIA. Their application depends on the actual legal arrangement and the current statutory provisions.

Understanding your internship agreement

Check the names of the parties; start and end dates; duties and learning objectives; supervisor or mentor; stipend and payment schedule; working hours and leave; performance expectations; confidentiality and information security; termination and complaints procedures; and applicable policies. Ask for unclear terms to be clarified in writing.

Internship, learnership and employment

An internship commonly provides workplace exposure and practical learning. A learnership is a structured programme governed by the Skills Development Act and associated requirements. Fixed-term employment and independent contracting are distinct arrangements. The document title alone does not settle legal status or statutory entitlements.

Where to seek assistance

Depending on the issue, consult HR, a programme coordinator, the Department of Employment and Labour, the CCMA, a union, a legal clinic or another appropriate body. Rights and responsibilities include lawful instructions, professional conduct, confidentiality, hazard reporting and responsible use of equipment.
