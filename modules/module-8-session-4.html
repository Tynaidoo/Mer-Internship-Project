Understanding the CCMA

The Commission for Conciliation, Mediation and Arbitration (CCMA) is an independent statutory body established under the Labour Relations Act. It resolves eligible labour disputes through conciliation, mediation and arbitration. It is not a general complaints office for every workplace problem; jurisdiction depends on the dispute, parties and applicable law.

When to approach the CCMA

Potential matters include unfair dismissal, qualifying unfair labour practices, certain wage or working-condition disputes and some discrimination disputes. The actual legal relationship matters: being called an intern does not alone establish whether the CCMA has jurisdiction. Internal discussions may be appropriate for routine misunderstandings, but serious harassment, threats, retaliation or safety issues may require prompt formal action.

Important referral deadlines

Common general referral periods are 30 days for unfair dismissal, 90 days for unfair labour practice and six months for certain unfair-discrimination disputes under the Employment Equity Act. The starting date and rules depend on the claim. Late referrals may require condonation, which is not automatic. An internal grievance does not necessarily stop statutory deadlines.

Referral process and evidence

Identify the dispute and relevant law; confirm the correct forum; obtain the prescribed form; serve it on the other party; submit the referral and proof of service; attend conciliation or other proceedings; and follow the next prescribed step. Preserve agreements, payslips, emails, dated notes, performance records and witness details lawfully.
