Module 6 · Session 3

Developing Your Professional CV

Develop a professional CV that clearly communicates your education, experience, skills and achievements.

CV • Experience • Skills • Career Development

Purpose of Session

This session helps you develop a professional CV that clearly communicates your education, experience, skills and achievements to a potential employer. As an intern, your education, internship responsibilities, projects, volunteering, extracurricular activities and other experiences can provide evidence of the skills and capabilities you have developed.

Learning Outcomes

  • Explain the purpose of a CV.
  • Identify the key sections of a professional CV.
  • Identify relevant skills and experiences to include.
  • Describe experiences using clear, action-focused statements.
  • Adapt your CV to a particular opportunity.
  • Review and improve your CV structure and content.
Spark

You Have More Experience Than You Think

Identify three experiences that helped you develop a skill. For each, ask: What did I do? What skill did I use or develop? How could I demonstrate this on my CV?

What Is a CV?

A CV communicates your education, experience, skills and achievements to a potential employer. Your experience can come from education, internships, projects, volunteering, part-time work, student activities, community involvement, training, extracurricular activities and other responsibilities.

Your CV Is Not One Document for Every Opportunity

Different opportunities require different combinations of knowledge, qualifications, experience, technical skills, interpersonal skills and capabilities. Adapt your CV to each opportunity by making your most relevant real experience easier for the employer to see—without changing or exaggerating it.

What Goes Into a CV?

The exact structure varies by industry, opportunity and experience level. For an intern or early-career professional, focus on clear contact information, a professional profile, education, internship and other relevant experience, skills and achievements.

Activity 2.1

What Does Your CV Say About You?

Review your existing CV, or think about what you would currently include. Imagine you are an employer seeing it for the first time.

  1. Can I quickly understand who this person is?
  2. Is their education clear?
  3. Can I identify relevant experience?
  4. Can I identify their skills?
  5. Do they provide evidence of what they can do?
  6. Is the information relevant?
  7. Is anything important missing?
  8. What would I improve first?

Activity 2.2

From Experience to Skills

Choose three experiences. For each identify what it was, what you did, the skill used, the result or outcome and how you could communicate it on your CV.

Writing About Your Experience

Avoid simply listing responsibilities. Use:

ACTION + TASK / CONTEXT + RESULT OR PURPOSE

Instead of: Responsible for research.
Consider: Conducted desktop research and summarised findings to support the development of a project report.

Using Action Verbs

Analysed • Assisted • Collaborated • Communicated • Coordinated • Created • Developed • Designed • Evaluated • Facilitated • Implemented • Organised • Planned • Prepared • Presented • Researched • Reviewed • Supported

Choose words that accurately describe what you did. Do not exaggerate your responsibility.

Activity 2.3

Writing Effective Experience Statements

Rewrite “Helped with a project.” Then choose one real internship responsibility and write an experience statement using the framework above.

Tailoring Your CV

Read the opportunity carefully and identify required qualifications, skills, experience, responsibilities, technical knowledge and other capabilities. Then match these requirements with evidence from your own experience.

Keep Your CV Professional

  • Use clear headings and consistent formatting.
  • Keep information concise and logically ordered.
  • Check spelling, grammar and dates.
  • Use enough white space for readability.
  • Keep all information accurate and honest.
Activity 2.4

Develop and Review Your CV

  1. Contact Information: Check that your details are professional and current.
  2. Professional Profile: Write or improve your short profile.
  3. Education: Ensure qualifications are accurate.
  4. Internship Experience: Add 2–4 action-focused statements.
  5. Other Experience: Add relevant employment, projects, volunteering or student activities.
  6. Skills: Include skills supported by evidence.
  7. Review: Ask whether your CV demonstrates what you can do, rather than only what you have done.

Checklist

  • ✓ I can explain the purpose of a CV.
  • ✓ My contact information is accurate and professional.
  • ✓ My education is clearly presented.
  • ✓ I have included my internship experience.
  • ✓ I have identified relevant experience beyond formal employment.
  • ✓ My skills are supported by real experiences.
  • ✓ I use action-focused statements.
  • ✓ My CV focuses on relevant information.
  • ✓ My CV is clearly structured and easy to read.
  • ✓ I have checked spelling and grammar.
  • ✓ All information is accurate and honest.
  • ✓ I understand that my CV should be adapted for different opportunities.

Reflection

“By the end of my internship, I would like to be able to add the following to my CV...” Identify one new skill, one meaningful project, one achievement, one new responsibility and one example of how you contributed in the workplace. Save your response in your Internship ePortfolio.
