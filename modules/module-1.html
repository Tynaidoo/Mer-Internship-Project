Module 1 • Session 1

Welcome to Your Internship Journey

Understand what an internship is, what to expect, what your responsibilities are, and how to make the most of the experience from your first day.

Welcome • Expectations • Responsibilities • Contribution

Purpose

To welcome interns into the workplace and help them understand what an internship is, why they are undertaking one, what they can expect, and what they can do to make the most of the experience.

Learning Outcomes

By the end of this session, interns will be able to:

  • Define an internship in their own words.
  • Identify realistic expectations for an internship.
  • Describe their basic responsibilities as an intern.
  • Identify behaviours and attitudes that can help them make the most of their internship.
  • Recognise that being an intern means being both a learner and a contributor.
Spark

Imagine that your internship is ending tomorrow. What would need to have happened for you to say, “That was a valuable experience”?

Welcome Message

Well done on reaching this exciting milestone in your professional journey.

Securing an internship is an important achievement. It reflects the dedication, hard work, and commitment you have demonstrated throughout your studies and your preparation for the world of work.

For many of you, an internship will be your first opportunity to experience a professional working environment. This can be both exciting and challenging. You may be entering a new organisation, meeting new people, working with colleagues from different backgrounds, and taking on responsibilities that are unfamiliar to you.

You will also need to adjust to the expectations of the workplace. The way you communicate, collaborate, solve problems, manage your time, and approach your responsibilities may be different from what you have experienced in an academic environment. An internship is an opportunity to put your knowledge into practice, learn from experienced professionals, develop new skills and discover more about yourself and your career interests.

The good news is that you are not expected to know everything from day one. An internship is a learning experience. You will make mistakes, ask questions, receive feedback, build relationships, and gradually become more confident in your abilities.

This course is designed to help you make the most of your internship. It will guide you through the professional skills, behaviours and practical knowledge you need to navigate the workplace successfully and turn your internship into a meaningful step towards your future career.

We are excited to have you with us!

What is an internship?

An internship is a structured opportunity to gain practical workplace experience while developing knowledge and skills.

Unlike a university assignment, an internship gives you the opportunity to apply what you have learned in a real-world environment.

Activity

My Internship Expectations

Record 2–3 answers under each heading.

1. What do I want to LEARN?

Examples: technical skills, communication, industry knowledge, project management.

2. What do I want to EXPERIENCE?

Examples: attending meetings, working on a project, presenting, collaborating with a team.

3. What do I want to CONTRIBUTE?

Examples: ideas, research, energy, problem-solving, technical knowledge.

What should you expect from your internship?

Every internship is different, but most interns can expect some combination of:

  • Meeting new people.
  • Having a supervisor or manager.
  • Receiving tasks and deadlines.
  • Attending meetings.
  • Learning new systems or tools.
  • Making mistakes and receiving feedback.
  • Working independently at times.
  • Working as part of a team.
  • Encountering things you have never done before.
  • Having questions — lots of them!
You are not expected to know everything. You are expected to be willing to learn.

The intern's basic responsibilities

Interns should generally:

  • Arrive prepared and on time.
  • Understand what is expected of them.
  • Complete assigned work.
  • Ask when they do not understand something.
  • Communicate when they encounter a problem.
  • Meet deadlines or communicate early if they cannot.
  • Be respectful to colleagues.
  • Follow company policies.
  • Protect confidential information.
  • Be willing to learn and receive feedback.
