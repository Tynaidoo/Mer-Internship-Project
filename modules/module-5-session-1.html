Module 5 · Session 1

Working Remotely and Virtually in Teams

Develop the communication, coordination, accountability and digital collaboration practices needed to work effectively in remote and virtual teams.

1 hour

Purpose

The world of work is changing, and technology has made it increasingly possible for people to work, communicate and collaborate from different locations. Remote and hybrid work require employees to approach teamwork differently from traditional face-to-face environments.

This session introduces interns to the knowledge, behaviours and practical strategies needed to become effective remote workers and virtual team members. It also encourages interns to think about how they can contribute to a positive, productive and accountable virtual team.

Learning Outcomes

  • Explain what it means to work remotely and participate effectively in a virtual team.
  • Apply practical strategies for managing your time, tasks and responsibilities while working remotely.
  • Demonstrate effective digital communication and collaboration practices.

What Is Remote and Virtual Work?

Remote work refers to work completed outside the traditional workplace, often using digital technologies to communicate and access work resources.

Virtual teams are teams whose members collaborate using digital communication technologies rather than relying primarily on face-to-face interaction.

Working virtually reduces informal conversations, spontaneous questions and physical proximity, so communication and coordination need to be more deliberate. For an intern, completing tasks is only part of the role: you also need to remain visible, communicate progress, ask for assistance, contribute to discussions and maintain professional relationships.

Working Effectively in Virtual Teams

Communication

Agree on platforms, response expectations, meetings, information sharing and availability.

Coordination

Clarify task ownership, deadlines, dependencies and where completed work is stored.

Trust

Meet commitments, communicate problems early, respect time and follow agreed processes.

Connection

Check in, celebrate achievements and make appropriate space for informal interaction.

Shared responsibility

Take initiative, share information, participate, offer assistance and own agreed tasks.

Activity 5.2.2

The Virtual Internship Team

Your four-person internship team is preparing a presentation due Friday. By Wednesday, Thabo has finished but not uploaded his section; Lerato is unclear on instructions but has not asked; Aisha's team-chat messages have gone unanswered; and Daniel has worked on a different file version because no shared location was agreed.

Answer the questions below.

1. What went wrong in the team's approach to remote working?

2. Which communication problems contributed to the situation?

3. Which responsibilities were unclear?

4. How could the team have prevented these problems?

5. What should the team do immediately?

6. What should the team agree on before starting their next project?

7. How should the team communicate if a member is struggling to complete a task?

8. What digital tools or communication channels would you recommend?

9. How can the team maintain trust and avoid blaming one another?
