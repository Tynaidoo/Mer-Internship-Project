Module 7 · Session 3

Hosting Workshops and Journal Clubs

Develop the skills to share knowledge, teach practical skills and facilitate research-based peer learning through workshops and journal clubs.

Peer Learning • Facilitation • Workshops • Journal Clubs

Purpose of Session

This session helps you develop the skills to share knowledge and facilitate learning through workshops and journal clubs. You will learn how to teach others a new skill or tool through a workshop and how to explore and discuss academic research through a journal club.

Learning Outcomes

  • Explain the difference between a workshop and a journal club.
  • Identify an appropriate topic for a peer-learning workshop.
  • Develop a clear learning outcome for a workshop.
  • Structure a practical workshop that allows participants to practise a new skill or tool.
  • Select an appropriate academic article for a journal club.
  • Identify and communicate the main points of an academic article.
  • Facilitate discussion and participation within a group.
  • Provide and receive constructive feedback following a workshop or journal club.
Spark

If You Learn Something Useful, Can You Teach It?

Think about something useful you have recently learned. If you had 10 minutes to teach it to another intern, what would they need to know first, what would you demonstrate, what would they practise, and how would you know whether they understood?

Two Types of Peer Learning

Workshops — Learning by Doing

A workshop focuses on teaching participants how to do something: a skill, tool or process. It is practical, includes demonstration and gives participants an opportunity to practise. The goal is: “I can now do this.”

Journal Clubs — Learning Through Research and Discussion

A journal club explores what research tells us about a topic. Participants investigate academic articles, share findings and discuss connections, differences and implications. The goal is: “I understand this topic better.”

WORKSHOP = SKILL DEVELOPMENT   •   JOURNAL CLUB = KNOWLEDGE + DISCUSSION + CRITICAL THINKING

Workshop or Journal Club?

Decide which format best suits each scenario:

  1. An intern demonstrates how to create interactive Power BI dashboards.
  2. The group investigates academic research on AI and workplace learning.
  3. An intern teaches the team to use a referencing tool.
  4. The group investigates research on future workplace skills.

Part 1: Hosting a Workshop

Start With a Learning Outcome

Ask: What should participants be able to do by the end? A useful learning outcome describes an action. Instead of “Understand Canva,” use “By the end of this workshop, participants should be able to create a simple professional infographic using Canva.”

Keep Your Workshop Focused

A short workshop should focus on one useful capability rather than everything you know about a tool. “How to create and use a PivotTable” is more manageable than “How to use Microsoft Excel.” A narrower goal creates more opportunity for practice.

A Simple Workshop Structure

INTRODUCE → DEMONSTRATE → PRACTISE → CHECK UNDERSTANDING → REFLECT
Activity 1.2

Design a Mini-Workshop

Choose one skill or tool you could realistically teach another intern.

Facilitating the Workshop

Being a facilitator is different from simply being the person who knows the most. Your role is to help other people learn.

  • Explain clearly and give appropriate instructions.
  • Check understanding and encourage questions.
  • Involve participants and manage time.
  • Support people who are struggling.
  • Allow participants to try things themselves.

Ask, Don't Only Tell

Questions such as “Where do you think we would find this option?”, “What do you think will happen if I change this setting?” and “Where could you use this in your internship?” help turn passive listeners into active learners.

Part 2: Hosting a Journal Club

A journal club is built around academic research and group discussion. Select a credible article relevant to the agreed topic and break it down into its essential contribution.

Activity 1.3

Breaking Down an Academic Article

Presenting Your Article

  1. Introduce the article: title, author, year and topic.
  2. Explain the research: what researchers wanted to understand and how they investigated it.
  3. Explain the main findings: focus on two or three important findings.
  4. Explain why it matters: connect it to the journal club topic.
  5. Ask a discussion question: invite the group to compare, question or apply the findings.

From Article Summaries to Discussion

A journal club should not become a sequence of individual presentations followed by everyone leaving. The value comes from comparing the articles and creating collective learning.

  • What ideas appeared across several articles?
  • Where did the articles agree or disagree?
  • Did they use different research methods or contexts?
  • What surprised the group?
  • What questions remain unanswered?
  • How might the findings relate to the internship or workplace?

Workshop Checklist

  • ✓ I have identified one clear skill or tool to teach.
  • ✓ I have created a clear learning outcome.
  • ✓ I can explain why the skill is useful.
  • ✓ I have planned a short demonstration and participant practice.
  • ✓ I have prepared and tested the required materials or technology.
  • ✓ I have planned how to check understanding.
  • ✓ I have allowed time for questions and know how much time I have.

Journal Club Checklist

  • ✓ The group has agreed on a clear topic.
  • ✓ I have selected a credible and relevant academic article.
  • ✓ I understand the purpose and method of the research.
  • ✓ I have identified the main findings and why they matter.
  • ✓ I have developed a discussion question.
  • ✓ I am prepared to listen to and discuss other articles.
  • ✓ I can connect my article with the broader journal club topic.
