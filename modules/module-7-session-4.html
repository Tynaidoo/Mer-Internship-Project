Module 7 · Session 4

Hosting Journal Clubs

Investigate workplace challenges through research, evidence, discussion and practical next steps.

Research • Evidence • Discussion • Synthesis

Purpose of Session

The purpose of this session is to help you develop the skills to investigate a real workplace challenge through research and evidence. You will learn how to define a topic, frame a problem, gather relevant sources, facilitate discussion and turn the discussion into useful findings and next steps.

Learning Outcomes

  • Define a clear topic, problem and key questions for a journal club.
  • Select and interpret relevant evidence from credible sources.
  • Present an article or source in a concise and useful way.
  • Facilitate discussion that leads to findings, gaps and next steps.

Time allocation: 60 minutes

Activities: 4.1 Topic, problem or question?; 4.2 Build the inquiry; 4.3 Break down the evidence; 4.4 Present and discuss; 4.5 From findings to next steps.

SPARK

Think of a challenge in your work or studies where people have opinions, but you are not sure what the evidence says. How could a group investigate it together rather than relying only on assumptions?

Part 1: What Is a Journal Club?

A journal club is a structured discussion in which a team uses evidence and research to investigate a real problem or challenge related to its work. The purpose is not simply to present papers, but to understand a challenge, explore what research says and identify insights that can inform practice, systems or decisions.

Unlike a workshop, which focuses on skill development and hands-on practice, a journal club focuses on knowledge-building, evidence evaluation, discussion and critical thinking.

Activity 4.1

Topic, problem or question?

Decide whether each statement is a topic, a problem or a key question.

Part 2: Topic → Problem → Questions

Start by identifying the topic, then define a specific problem or challenge and develop key questions to guide the search for evidence. This keeps the session focused and makes it easier to select useful sources.

Topic → Problem → Questions → Evidence → Discussion → Findings / Next Steps

The problem should matter to the team but be focused enough to investigate meaningfully. Questions should clarify what evidence is needed.

Activity 4.2

Build the inquiry

In pairs, turn a broad workplace topic into a focused journal club inquiry.

Part 3: Gathering and Evaluating Evidence

Each participant should identify and review a relevant source that helps answer one or more questions. Sources may include academic papers, research reports, case studies and credible articles. Consider relevance, credibility, publication date, context and strength of evidence.

Record the title, author and year; what the source investigated; how the evidence was gathered; the main findings; limitations; and why it matters for the journal club topic. These notes help you present and compare sources.

Part 4: Presenting and Facilitating Discussion

When presenting a source, do not explain every section in detail. Help the group understand what it investigated, how, its main findings and why they matter.

Move the discussion beyond individual summaries. Ask where sources agree, differ, which evidence seems strongest, what gaps remain and how findings might inform practice. Distinguish evidence from opinion and work toward shared understanding.

Activity 4.4

Present and discuss

Share a short evidence summary with a partner or small group, then discuss.

Part 5: Turning Discussion Into Findings and Next Steps

A strong journal club ends with a documented outcome. Identify what was learned, what remains uncertain, gaps in the evidence and next steps. These might involve testing an approach, further research, revising a process or investigating a new question.

Where possible, record the topic, problem, key questions, source summaries, findings and actions in a shared location so the learning can be reused.
